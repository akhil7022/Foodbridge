02 The problem

It was never a shortage.
It was always a timer.

The world grows more food than it eats. Yet a fifth of what reaches consumers is thrown out while 733 million people go hungry. The bottleneck isn't supply and it isn't generosity — it's that cooked food has a brutally short legal window to be safely handed over, and coordination by phone call is slower than the bacteria.

04 The handoff

Three steps.
One unbroken chain.

No phone chains, no "who's collecting it?" Nobody is allowed to be uncertain about whose job it is at any moment.

01

Donor · 40 seconds

Post what's left

Food type, kilograms, prepared-at time and pickup address. The form refuses anything already past its safe window, so unsafe listings can't exist.

  • ▸ Photo + temperature log optional
  • ▸ Recurring schedule for repeat surplus
02

Engine · under 1 second

Ranked, not spammed

Every partner inside your radius is scored on distance, capacity, food-type fit, response speed and cold-chain. The top match gets the alert first — not all twelve at once.

  • ▸ Auto re-ranks as the window shrinks
  • ▸ Escalates if no accept in 8 minutes
03

Handoff · on arrival

Prove it, then log it

Timestamped photo, arrival temperature and a meal count signed off by the NGO. That record is what protects the donor — and what goes on their certificate.

  • ▸ kg diverted · meals served · CO₂e avoided
  • ▸ Exportable impact receipt

NGO verification

Registration, 12A/80G, kitchen hygiene and delivery history checked before a partner can claim food.

Live status tracker

Listed → Accepted → In transit → Delivered. One timeline shared by donor, NGO and courier.

Recurring surplus

A bakery with predictable 6 PM surplus auto-posts daily. Shelters plan instead of react.

Food-safety guardrails

Expired windows blocked at entry, high-risk categories flagged, full audit trail per handoff.
